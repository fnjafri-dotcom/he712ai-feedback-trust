Feedback and Trust in AI

This week you'll commit to a prediction, work through four readings with a short activity for each, test your own trust in AI feedback, then reflect on what changed. The difficulties are deliberate. Committing to an answer before you know it, and working to retrieve and apply ideas, makes learning stick. These are what researchers call desirable difficulties.

2 · Read and work: desirable difficulties

For each reading: do the short activity, answer the shared question, then compare with the instructors' notes. Total reading time is about 1 hour 45 minutes.

Reading 1 · The power of feedback

35 min

Hattie J, Timperley H. Rev Educ Res. 2007;77(1):81-112. doi:10.3102/003465430298487. Read the feedback model (the three questions and four levels). Skim the rest.

Activity: sort the feedback

Assign each piece of feedback to Hattie and Timperley's level, then check your answers.

In the Talwalkar notes, AI does well with task-level and process-level feedback that's tied to clear criteria. Self-regulation feedback depends on a back-and-forth conversation, which is exactly where AI struggled as a debriefer (Reading 2). Self-level praise is the least useful level whoever gives it.

Shared question: Which feedback level is AI best at, and which is it worst at? Why?

Reading 2 · AI-powered simulation for telehealth communication training

20 min

Jafri FN, Elsener M, Kumar A, Kardong-Edgren S, Edwards RA. Simul Healthc. 2026. doi:10.1097/SIH.0000000000000940. Read the debriefing findings and the discussion.

Activity: predict before you read

The team tried several debriefing approaches across AI platforms. Which do you think AI could deliver reliably? Check all that apply, then read and reveal.

Across platforms, AI couldn't reliably deliver advocacy-inquiry or plus-delta. Rubric-focused microdebriefing worked best, because it plays to AI's strength in consistent, structured feedback and avoids the complex facilitation it can't yet do. A companion study found communication scores on real calls improved 19 to 37% after the training.

Shared question: Why couldn't AI do advocacy-inquiry, and what does that tell us about which feedback needs a person?

Reading 3 · Ambient AI scribes for educational feedback notes

30 min

Talwalkar JS, Chartash D, Zhang L, et al. JMIR Med Educ. 2026;12:e89996. doi:10.2196/89996. Whole paper (11 pages).

Activity: estimate before you read

An AI scribe recorded instructors' spoken feedback to first-year medical students, and an AI model wrote it up as a note. Make your best guesses.

What share of the AI's feedback points misrepresented what the instructor said? 10%

What share described things that never happened (hallucinations)? 10%

How much longer were the AI-assisted notes than human-only notes? 2×


Raters also scored the AI-assisted notes higher on feedback quality (median 3 vs 2 out of 5), and instructors caught most errors while editing. But no one asked the students whether the notes helped them.

Shared question: The AI notes scored higher, but no one asked the students. What would you need to know before using this in your course?

Reading 4 · Ethical use of AI in health professions education

20 min

Masters K. AMEE Guide No. 158. Med Teach. 2023. doi:10.1080/0142159X.2023.2186203. Read four sections only: algorithm bias, algorithm transparency, responsibility and accountability, and autonomy.

Activity: you decide

A program adopts an AI tool that gives nursing students feedback on medication calculations. The tool tells a student a dose calculation is correct. It isn't. The student repeats the error in a high-stakes simulation and fails the station.

Who is most responsible?

There's no single right answer, and that's Masters' point. AI offers probabilities, not answers for an individual case, so a person must make the final call, and institutions need clear policies on responsibility, accountability, blame, and credit before something goes wrong. He also names a trap: faculty can be blamed for ignoring an approved tool and for blindly following it. You'll meet that tension again in the lab.

Shared question: If an AI feedback tool gets something wrong, who is responsible in your setting?

3 · Trust Lab

Five short cases, each with AI feedback on a learner's work. For each one, decide what you'd do with it: use it as is, edit it, check something first, or not use it. There's no answer key. After each choice you'll see a case for using it and a case for caution, and at the end you'll see your own pattern to bring to your partner. (About 15 minutes.)
